The Fleet page is where a claimed carrier manages its model listings on
[Airside](/features/airside), the self-serve provider console. Each listing is
a "flight strip" showing its status, its currently approved pricing, and any
tariff filing still awaiting review.

## Listing statuses

| Status                              | Meaning                                                                   |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Filed**                           | Drafted — waiting for the initial price filing to be approved             |
| **In service**                      | Approved and routable as `<provider>/<model>`; billed at the filed prices |
| **Paused**                          | In service but taken out of routing by you until resumed                  |
| **Rejected**                        | The initial filing was rejected; fix the price and file again             |
| **Delisted**                        | Retired — kept in history, no longer routable                             |
| **Awaiting clearance / Fare filed** | A filing (initial or price update) is pending review                      |

## Registering an aircraft

Click **Register aircraft**, pick the carrier (when your company holds more
than one), and fill in the model id, display name, family, context size, capabilities,
accepted `tool_choice` modes, optional quantization, and the initial tariff.
For a model already in the catalogue, **Use catalog price** copies another
provider's flat prices as a starting point. Prices are per token and accept exponent notation —
`2e-6` reads as $2 per million tokens.

## Changing a price

Pricing is never edited in place. **File fare** opens a tariff filing with the
new input/output prices; the model keeps serving at its current approved
pricing until the filing is approved. Only one filing per model can be pending
at a time, and every filing — with its review outcome and notes — is listed in
the console's filings history.

## Pausing a model

**Pause** takes an in-service model out of routing without review: it stops
receiving new requests within about a minute and keeps its approved pricing,
pending filings, and history. **Resume** puts it back in service the same way.
Filings approved while paused apply without resuming the model.

For a model imported from the built-in catalogue, pausing behaves like
delisting: requests fall back to the catalogue entry until you resume.

## Removing a model

Deleting a drafted or rejected listing removes it outright. An in-service
model is **delisted** instead: it stops routing immediately but stays in your
history and analytics.

<Callout type="warning">
	Delisting takes effect within about a minute — in-flight requests finish, but
	new requests for the model are rejected.
</Callout>
