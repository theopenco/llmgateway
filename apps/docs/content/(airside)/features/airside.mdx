[Airside](https://airside.llmgateway.io) is the self-serve console for the companies behind the models — the [providers](https://llmgateway.io/providers) LLM Gateway routes to. It runs on an airport metaphor: LLM Gateway is the airport, developers are passengers, and providers are **carriers**. As a carrier you claim your provider, register your fleet of models, file your fares, and watch traffic arrive.

## Claiming your carrier

Sign up at [airside.llmgateway.io](https://airside.llmgateway.io) with your **company email** and verify it. A catalogue provider is claimable when the registrable domain of your verified email matches the registrable domain of the provider's API endpoint (or its website) — if your API is served from `api.example.ai`, an `@example.ai` address can claim it. Subdomains collapse to the registrable domain, so `ops@mail.example.ai` works too.

If your email is on a different domain than your API, prove the API's domain over DNS instead. On the onboarding page, add each domain under your company and publish the shown TXT record on `_llmgateway-airside.<domain>`. Every domain verified this way counts like your email domain for claiming, registering a carrier, and crew invites. The onboarding page lists each domain with how it was proven: over DNS, or by a verified email when a claim was matched on your email domain. An email proof belongs to the person who filed the claim, so only DNS-verified domains carry over to the rest of your crew.

Every claim is reviewed by the LLM Gateway team before the carrier goes live; a rejected claim shows the review note so you can follow up. One company can operate several carriers — regional deployments or separate brands all live under one console.

Listing on llmgateway.io carries a one-time **$2,500 listing fee** per provider company, paid during onboarding. Providers we already work with receive an **invite code** that waives it — entered in the same onboarding step instead of paying. See the [pricing summary](https://airside.llmgateway.io/pricing.md) for the full economics.

## Crew

A listing covers your whole team. Under **Crew**, company owners invite teammates by email — up to **10 members** per company, pending invites included. Invites are limited to your team's domains (the ones that prove your claims); a teammate with an existing account joins instantly, anyone else joins the first time they sign in with the invited address.

## Registering a new carrier

Not in the [provider catalogue](https://llmgateway.io/providers) yet? Register your provider as a **new carrier** from the onboarding page: pick a carrier id, display name, and the base URL of your **OpenAI-compatible API** (the gateway calls `<base URL>/v1/chat/completions`, unless a listing picks a different [upstream API](#upstream-api-and-preflight)). Enter the base URL only: a URL whose path contains `/v1` or `/chat/completions` is rejected, because the gateway appends the endpoint path itself and the doubled path would fail every request. The endpoint must live on your verified email's domain or a domain your company verified over DNS — the same anti-squatting rule as claiming — and registrations go through the same review. Once approved, your carrier appears on the public providers and models pages and its listings route like any other provider's.

## Listing your fleet

Once your claim is active, register models under **Fleet**: model name, display name, description, family, context size, max output, capability flags (streaming, vision, audio input, tools, JSON output, reasoning), the supported `reasoning_effort` tiers, the `tool_choice` modes your endpoint accepts, and optionally the weight **quantization** you serve (`int4`, `int8`, `fp4`, `fp6`, `fp8`, `fp16`, `bf16`, or `fp32`). Approved quantization shows on the Fleet card and the public model card. If your API expects a different id than the one developers use, set the **upstream model ID**; like the upstream API, it is fixed once the model is listed.

A listing can also carry optional **rate limits** in requests per minute and per day. Choose whether the cap is shared **across all organizations** (the total traffic sent to your deployment stays under it) or applies **per organization** (each organization gets its own counter, so total load grows with the number of customers). Platform-set limits always take precedence. A new listing is created as a draft together with an **initial price filing**; when the LLM Gateway team approves that filing, the model goes active.

Registering a model that already exists in the catalogue reuses its identity: display name, description, and family come from the catalogue model, and **Use catalog price** copies another provider's flat prices into your initial tariff as a starting point. For a new model, the family field suggests existing catalogue families.

### Context window

Declare the context window your endpoint really accepts. For a model the catalogue or another carrier already serves, it must be within 10% of the largest window the model is served with (its **full window**). The fleet form checks this while you type: a context size outside that range shows a warning with the model's full window and cannot be saved or submitted, and the API refuses it as well. Editing an existing listing requires bringing it into range. The **context size** preflight check then confirms your endpoint really accepts it.

The same rule decides routing. A request that names a model without a provider prefix (for example `deepseek-v4-flash`) only goes to deployments within 10% of the model's full window, as soon as at least one provider serves that window, so a long conversation never lands on a deployment that cannot hold it. Requests that name your carrier (for example `your-carrier/deepseek-v4-flash`) still reach a smaller deployment.

Listings accepted before this rule can sit below the model's full window. They keep serving requests that name your carrier, but requests without a provider prefix skip them. To get back into that traffic, serve at least 90% of the model's full window and update the listing's context size. LLM Gateway may delist a listing that stays below the full window; the fleet page then shows it as **Delisted by LLM Gateway**, and you can request a relist once preflight passes. LLM Gateway reviews the request; once approved, the listing is back in service.

Draft listings save immediately. On a live listing, a metadata change (description, context size, capabilities, quantization, and so on) is filed for review and applies once approved. Saving again while that change is pending replaces it in place, and saving the live values withdraws it. A pending price filing blocks metadata edits until it is reviewed.

Carriers claiming a catalogue provider can also **import their catalogue models** in one click: each active catalogue model becomes a managed listing with its current price as an approved filing. Once approved, the listing serves the model at its filed fares and keeps the catalogue entry's request handling. Pausing or delisting an imported model takes it out of service instead of falling back to the catalogue entry; an approved relist or resuming puts it back. The LLM Gateway team can then remove the provider's static definition and mappings; only its active Airside listings remain available.

### Catalogue removal

While the static provider definition exists, routing and BYOK key validation use
its endpoint configuration. An Airside base URL configured in advance takes effect
after that definition is removed; a BYOK key's explicit base URL still overrides it.

When a provider's static catalogue definition is removed, its existing active
Airside claim serves from its configured base URL, managed credentials, and
active listings. Models without an active Airside listing are no longer offered by that provider.

The carrier's declared profile then drives compliance routing. Later profile
edits apply directly; missing facts do not satisfy compliance requirements.
Provider IDs, BYOK keys, approved prices, and historical usage retain their
existing identity. Configure the carrier endpoint before adding managed serving
credentials. Revoking the claim stops its listings from serving while preserving
the catalogue provider record and platform-owned credentials.

### Upstream API and preflight

Each listing also picks the **upstream API** the gateway speaks to it: the carrier default, OpenAI Chat Completions, OpenAI Responses, or Google Vertex `generateContent`. The choice is independent of the capabilities you declare — tool calls, JSON output, vision, reasoning, and web search are all probed and served through whichever API you picked, so pick the one your endpoint actually implements. It is fixed once the model is listed.

Before a listing can be filed, Airside runs a **preflight** against your endpoint through that API: one check per declared capability, plus one for each declared limit. The **context size** check first measures how your endpoint tokenizes a short sample, then sends a prompt filling about 90% of the declared window, up to 2 million tokens, and fails if your endpoint refuses it or reports processing less than 80% of the window; the **max output** check requests the full declared output budget and fails if your endpoint refuses it. A listing with a **cached input price**, default or regional, also gets a **prompt caching** check: it repeats one prompt of about 6,000 tokens up to five times over roughly 20 seconds and fails unless a response reports cached input tokens (`usage.prompt_tokens_details.cached_tokens` on Chat Completions). Cached input is billed and routed from that count, so either report cache hits or file without a cached price. A cached price above half of its input price is flagged in the fare form and to our reviewers, since cached input usually costs far less. A refusal is reported with the probe that was sent followed by your endpoint's own answer. A request that times out is retried up to twice; a check that passes on a retry still passes, with a warning that your endpoint may be slow or overloaded. Some checks are **optional** for now: a response that misses one still passes its check, with the miss shown as a warning marked **Optional**. Optional checks may become required later. These are currently optional:

- **Basic completion** requires `usage` with positive input and output token counts, cached tokens counted within the input, and, on Chat Completions, a response `id`.
- **Streaming** requires a usage report (on Chat Completions, honour `stream_options.include_usage` with a final usage chunk). The streamed input token count must match the non-streaming request for the same prompt; usage sent as per-chunk increments is flagged, because the last reported value is what gets billed.
- **Reasoning** requires evidence that the model reasoned: reasoning text, a reasoning block or reasoning tokens in `usage`. An endpoint that accepts `reasoning_effort` but ignores it is flagged.

OpenAI Chat Completions listings, including custom carriers using their default API, also have a required **message content compatibility** check. It sends multi-part text arrays in system, assistant, and user messages separately, repeating each case with streaming when declared. Rejecting a valid array fails preflight even when optional checks only warn; the results identify the role and streaming mode that failed. Older runs without this check must be repeated before submitting a new listing, widening its claims, or requesting a relist.

A stream must already contain assistant text and a finish reason, or the **streaming** check fails. The **tool calls** check is required too: on Chat Completions, a response with `tool_calls` must finish with `finish_reason: "tool_calls"` (a named `tool_choice` may finish with `stop`), or the check fails, because clients stop instead of running the tool. Since no other `tool_choice` mode fixes this, it never narrows your modes.

The context check is billed like any other large prompt. You supply a provider API key that can call the model, saved as this carrier's **test key** under Settings so later runs — yours and ours — reuse it. Use a key separate from the one behind your live integration: preflight traffic is billed by your own platform and is not tracked in LLMGateway usage or billing. You can replace or remove it at any time. Editing the model, its limits included, invalidates the run, so a listing never ships with checks made against different settings.

Re-verifying a live listing reports, it does not rewrite: a failed check names the capability your endpoint refused and leaves the listing exactly as you declared it, because one refused request is as easily a transient fault as a missing capability. Fix the endpoint and verify again, or switch the capability off yourself if the deployment genuinely does not do it. Fleet marks the listing **In service · unverified** until a run passes again.

A capability you have edited on a live listing lives in a filing until we approve it, and preflight verifies **that** shape rather than the one currently serving — otherwise a run would pass without ever exercising the capability you are trying to prove. A failed check is reported next to the filing rather than removed from it; the reviewer decides.

Each check lists the upstream requests it actually sent. A check can be more than one request: the tool check walks the `tool_choice` modes and the reasoning check sweeps the effort tiers, so every attempt appears nested under its check with its own pass or fail and the upstream message on failure. The breakdown fills in while the run is still going. A check fails only when every one of its requests failed.

Where a check can tell the difference between a capability and a dialect, it narrows the listing itself. A `tool_choice` mode your endpoint mishandles is dropped while tool calls stay on, and a `reasoning_effort` tier your endpoint rejects outright is retried at another tier — reasoning stays on and only the rejected tiers come off your declared set. If one tier is rejected the check goes on to try the others, since a tier left declared is one the gateway will forward verbatim. So a deployment implementing only a subset of the tiers verifies without losing reasoning, and you do not have to know which subset in advance.

### Daily verification and regression alerts

When enabled by the operator, the worker rechecks every live chat and typed-decision mapping across the [catalogue](https://llmgateway.io/models), including regional deployments and Airside listings. This uses the same declared-capability and limit checks as preflight, including its optional-check policy. Image, video, audio-only, embedding, moderation, reranking, and other non-chat outputs are not supported by this verifier and are not scheduled.

The worker scans on startup and every five minutes, queuing at most one scheduled run per mapping per UTC calendar day across all replicas. It does not backfill missed days. An existing queued or running verification delays scheduling until a later scan; manual runs take priority, and scheduled runs rotate between providers with at most one running per provider. A large queue can therefore finish after the day it was scheduled.

Only active providers, models, and mappings qualify. Deactivated mappings and paused, draft, rejected, or delisted Airside listings are excluded. Eligibility and the current declaration are checked again before execution and alert delivery. If the declaration changed or a usable key disappeared, the run is retained as failed with skipped checks, not reported as a capability regression. Completed runs remain in the existing verification history. Daily runs never delist models or change capabilities, prices, tool-choice modes, or reasoning tiers.

Claimed providers use their current saved **test key**, never their managed serving key. Unclaimed catalogue mappings use an active managed key allowed for the model, or the provider credential configured on the worker. Terminal history ciphertext is never reused; rotating or removing a test key takes effect on the next execution. Providers without a usable credential are skipped. Verification requests consume upstream resources, including large context-window probes, without appearing in gateway usage or billing.

For self-hosting, configure these worker environment variables:

- `AIRSIDE_DAILY_VERIFICATION_ENABLED=true` enables scheduling; it is disabled by default. Turning it off stops new scheduled runs; already queued runs continue to drain.
- `MODEL_VERIFICATION_CONCURRENCY` sets parallel verification consumers per worker replica (1–16; default 4). Scheduled runs still serialize per provider across replicas; manual runs are prioritized but do not interrupt an in-flight verification.
- `AIRSIDE_VERIFICATION_DISCORD_WEBHOOK_URL` enables Discord regression alerts independently of scheduling. Leave it unset to disable delivery. Configure the usual database, encryption secret, and provider credentials on the worker too. No external cron job or database migration is needed.

An alert requires a failed check or probe that previously passed for the same deployment and compatible declaration. The most recent observed result is the baseline: unchanged persistent failures do not alert every day, recovery re-arms detection, and new or optional checks do not generate regression alerts without a prior pass. Existing manual and carrier verification history can supply that baseline.

Alerts contain only provider/model identifiers, region, check IDs, verification ID, and completion time; they omit credentials, prompts, upstream feedback, and responses, and disable mentions. Delivery has a five-second timeout. Network and HTTP failures retain retry state in the existing job-progress table, retry after at least five minutes, and respect `Retry-After` up to seven days. New alert candidates are discovered for seven days after queueing; previously attempted deliveries keep retrying after that discovery window. Successful sends are durably deduplicated across replicas. A crash after Discord accepts a message but before the database commits can still duplicate that message.

### Carrier verification emails

Set `AIRSIDE_VERIFICATION_EMAIL_ENABLED=true` on the worker to email active, verified carrier team members at their registered addresses. This is independent of Discord and requires the existing production email configuration (`RESEND_API_KEY`); it is disabled by default and does not send real mail outside production.

A failure message aggregates currently failing live listings and is sent at most once per rolling 24 hours per carrier account. A separate recovery message is sent after subsequent passing verification confirms the still-active previously reported listings are healthy again. Missing, skipped, or removed listings do not prove recovery, and a declaration change alone does not either: the current declaration must subsequently pass verification. These transactional messages contain listing identifiers and check names, never provider credentials, prompts, responses, or upstream error messages.

Delivery attempts and the cooldown survive worker restarts and replicas. Email sends have a ten-second deadline; timeouts are treated as ambiguous delivery, not success. Retries reuse the email provider’s idempotency key. Stale attempts, changed recipients, and changed listing eligibility retire the old attempt rather than send an outdated message. An ambiguous failure attempt conservatively reserves the rolling 24-hour quota from its last dispatch; retired attempts are not treated as confirmed delivery or recovery evidence. Attempts older than the provider’s 24-hour idempotency window are not resent with that key; operators can reconcile uncertain delivery without blocking future eligible notifications.

### Web search format

A listing that declares **web search** also picks the shape its Chat Completions endpoint takes a search in:

- **`web_search_options`** — OpenAI's Chat Completions body field, `{"web_search_options": {"search_context_size": "low"}}`. It has no forced mode; the model decides.
- **`tools: [{type: "web_search"}]`** — a hosted-tool entry, the Responses API's shape, which some chat endpoints accept instead. A request that requires a search sends `tool_choice: "required"` when web search is its only tool and your listing accepts `required`; next to the caller's own functions it leaves `tool_choice` to the model.

The gateway translates every caller's web search — a `web_search` tool, `web_search_options`, or a Responses API tool — into the shape you pick, and passes `url_citation` annotations back. A search is billed only on evidence in your response: `url_citation` annotations, a usage counter (`server_tool_use.web_search_requests` or `num_search_queries`), or `prompt_tokens` beyond what the request could encode to (the retrieved pages you added to the prompt). The **web search** preflight check sends its probe in the chosen shape and passes only on the same evidence, so a shape your endpoint ignores fails it. Changing the shape on a listing with web search needs a passing preflight in the new shape.

### Decision models

Set **Model type** to **Typed decisions** to list a decision model: one that answers typed questions with probabilities instead of generating text. Developers call it on LLM Gateway's [`/v1/decisions`](/features/decisions) endpoint, and the gateway forwards the request to `<base URL>/v1/decisions` on your API with `input` and `questions` as sent and `model` set to your upstream model ID. Your endpoint must implement OpenAI's Decisions API:

- Accept `POST /v1/decisions` with `model` (your upstream model ID), `input` (a string, or user messages with `input_text` and `input_image` parts), and `questions` (`predicate`, `choice`, and `score`).
- Return one answer per question in question order, echoing each `name`: a `probability` from 0 to 1 for `predicate`; a `choice` from the offered values with one entry in `probabilities` per value for `choice`; a `score` from 0 to the highest level index with one entry in `probabilities` per level for `score`.
- Report `usage.input_tokens` as a non-negative integer. Decisions are billed on input tokens only, plus an optional per-request price, so a decision listing files an output price of 0 and takes no cached or regional fares. A response without a usable count is billed on an estimate of the request size.

A decision listing declares only **image input** and its maximum input tokens; chat capabilities do not apply. Preflight sends one request per question type and fails unless each response has that shape and reports a positive `usage.input_tokens`. With image input declared, it also sends a solid red image and fails unless your model answers that it is red with a probability of at least 0.5. Carriers whose catalogue decision API is not `/v1/decisions` cannot list decision models.

The model type is fixed once listed. While any listing of a model ID is in service, the ID stays a chat model or a decision model, and a catalogue model keeps its catalogue type. A model ID without a provider prefix goes to the catalogue mapping when there is one, otherwise to the listing with the lowest input price.

## Price filings

Listed prices are what developers are billed, so pricing never changes silently: it only changes through a reviewed **price filing**.

- The **initial** filing sets the launch pricing and activates the model on approval.
- Every later price change is an **update** filing — drafted by you, approved by the LLM Gateway team, and only then in effect.

A filing covers input, output, optional cached-input, and optional per-request prices (token prices in USD per million tokens, the per-request price in USD), plus a note for the reviewer. It can also carry **regional fares** — per-region price overrides developers reach with `<provider>/<model>:<region>`, while all other traffic pays the default fares. Each approved filing's regional set fully replaces the previous one, and while no filing is pending a region can be dropped from the Fleet page — removing an offering changes no price, so it applies immediately without review. The latest approved filing is the model's effective price; pending and rejected filings are tracked under **Filings**.

## Routing and billing

Approved listings are genuinely routable. A request for `<provider>/<model>` that does not match the static catalogue resolves against active Airside listings with an approved price filing, and is billed at the filed prices — no extra configuration on the developer side.

### Getting paid

LLM Gateway settles the traffic it sends you under one of three billing modes. The mode is not self-serve: we discuss it with you and set it up together before your carrier goes live. **Settings → Billing** shows the mode in place; contact us to change it. In every mode we reconcile your charges against our own traffic records each month.

1. **Pay-as-you-go** — we open an account on your platform with a card on auto top-up, so usage is charged as it happens.
2. **Payout** — you issue us an API key and invoice us on the 1st for the previous month. We pay out your earnings by USD wire, weekly or monthly.
3. **Post-billing** — we open an account on your platform and you switch it to post-paid. You invoice us on the 1st for the previous month and we pay once a month.

## Traffic

The **Traffic** page shows usage of your claimed providers: requests, errors, input/output/total tokens, and billed traffic in USD, as a daily series and broken down per model.

Chart axes and compact counts use `k`, `M`, `B`, or `T` for thousands through trillions, so large traffic volumes fit within the chart.

## Incidents

The **Incidents** page lists every mapping of your claimed providers that failed requests in the selected window (1 hour to 3 days), with its error rate, error count, upstream/gateway split, and total requests. Only upstream and gateway errors count: both are retried on another key or provider, and upstream errors count against your uptime. Client errors, canceled requests, and content-filtered requests are excluded.

By default the page counts only traffic served by the gateway's own credentials. Turn on **Bring your own key traffic** to also include requests customers sent with their own provider keys.

Expand a row to see the top error shapes behind it: HTTP status, status text, a response excerpt, the cause, the gateway's classification, and whether the request was streamed. The shapes cover every error in the window, including responses that started successfully and failed midway, up to the latest 100,000 per mapping. Counts always include retried attempts; the **Retried errors in details** switch drops retried attempts from the error shapes only.

Switch to **By error type** to group the same errors across mappings instead: each error shows its total, its streaming and non-streaming counts, and every model it hit with per-model counts. Use it to spot failures such as timeouts that are not specific to one model. The same per-mapping limit applies.

Both views group errors that differ only by request-specific values: ids, hashes, UUIDs, and numbers of four or more digits show as `<id>`, `<uuid>`, and `<n>` in the excerpt. Numbers after a hyphen, such as dated model names, are kept. Turn off **Group similar errors** to see each exact response instead.

To focus on one mapping, pick it from the mapping filter or open `/dashboard/incidents?mapping=<provider>/<model>[:<region>]`. The **Errors** column on Traffic and the **Incidents** link on each active Fleet listing open the page pre-filtered.

## Fares and routing

Under **Fares** each claimed provider has two knobs:

- **Traffic discount** (0–50%) — a discount you offer on routed traffic.
- **Landing fee** (5–50%) — the gateway margin you accept; the baseline is **20%**.

Accepting more than the baseline margin or offering a discount makes your traffic effectively cheaper, which boosts you in the [smart routing election](/features/routing); accepting less prices you up. Like price changes, fare changes are filed for review and only reach routing once the LLM Gateway team approves them. The election scores every candidate provider on weighted factors — expected token cost after your discount and margin (`%routing.weights.price%`), availability/uptime (`%routing.weights.uptime%`), throughput (`%routing.weights.throughput%`), and latency (`%routing.weights.latency%`) — and the lowest score wins. Cache-read savings count toward token cost; cache support alone receives no additional preference by default.

<Callout type="info">
	Routing reacts to live metrics: reliability and speed still matter. A cheap
	but flaky deployment loses the election to a slightly pricier, stable one.
</Callout>

## Settings

**Settings** holds the preflight **test key** for each claimed carrier, shown masked. Replace or remove it at any time; without one, preflight asks for a key on every run.

Settings also shows each carrier's **billing mode**. It is view-only: the mode is agreed with the LLM Gateway team and set up before your carrier goes live (see [Getting paid](#getting-paid)). Contact the LLM Gateway team to change it.
